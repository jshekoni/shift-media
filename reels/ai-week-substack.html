A friend asked me recently how she should start with AI. She uses ChatGPT the way most people do: she asks it the odd question, gets it to tidy up a letter, and leaves it there. She is hoping to build a business of her own, and she wanted to know where to begin. I realised, as I tried to answer, that almost everyone I know is standing exactly where she is, at the door of something enormous and using it as a slightly cleverer search engine.

What I told her is what I have done myself over the past year, and it has changed how I run my working life.

Your first week, in five steps

1. Start with a project, not a question

Most people talk to AI in one long, scattered conversation, then wonder why it never seems to remember anything. Create a project instead: a dedicated space for one area of your life, whether that is a new business, your family admin or a legal matter you are trying to get on top of. Everything about that subject happens inside it, so the context builds rather than evaporates.

2. Hire your staff

Inside that project, open a new chat and give it a role. It sounds almost too simple, it works. Tell it who it is ("You are my head of staff, my partner in this new business") and it steps into that role. Then talk it through what you want to build: who it is for, where they are, what problem you solve, whether you work one to one or run free workshops that lead into a paid programme, how long that programme should be and what you might charge. If you would rather not type, speak, or ask it to interview you.

Then ask it who else you need. In my case the answer was a researcher and a website builder, and later someone for marketing. Each becomes its own chat, with its own name, because names make them easy to remember and easy to direct. The researcher finds everything that already exists in your field: the competitors, the courses, the prices. The website builder takes that research, looks at sites you admire and builds a first version for you to react to. Before long you have the foundations of a business, and a team working at doctoral level, around the clock, for almost nothing.

3. Give it your life to work with

AI is only as useful as what it can see, and the most valuable thing you have is your own experience: your files, your notes, your history. That is what turns a generic assistant into one that can build a programme, a booklet or a product out of what you actually know.

I connected mine to my email. Every morning it goes through my inbox, sends me a report of everything that matters and drafts the replies it can handle, which wait for me to check and send. I still open my email myself, for the small hit of it, but nothing gets missed. It also searches each day for press and podcast opportunities and drafts the pitches.

The speed of it is hard to convey until you see it. When the people who repaired my dishwasher damaged my floor, I needed new boards, a quote, their agreement and then an invoice for the work. I asked the AI to find the original supplier in my emails, gave it the measurements and asked it to draft a request for a quote. When the quote came back, it prepared the email to the repair company. My builder doesn't produce invoices, so it made one for him, attached it and sent it. When they asked for proof the work was done and paid for, it produced a final invoice and sent that too. The money is now in my account, and at no point did the whole thing feel like a chore.

People often ask me about boundaries, and I understand why. But every boundary you set is something the AI can no longer help you with. The way I see it, Google's own AI is already in most people's email. You can't stop that, so you might as well choose the tools that work for you and put them to use.

4. Build a second brain

Give everything a single home. Google Drive becomes what people call a second brain: a hub where every document lives and where the AI can find anything in seconds. Mine was a mess. I asked it to sort everything into folders, create an archive and tidy up years of stray files, and it did. Now I can ask it to choose three photographs from a particular folder, add them to a booklet and turn that into a carousel, and it knows where to look.

5. Connect the plumbing

The AI will walk you through the rest: buying your domain, setting up a professional email address, connecting your website so that changes go live the moment you approve them, and linking a scheduling tool so it can post to social media for you. My website builder can create three new pages, and when I say yes, they are live. Nothing is dragged, uploaded or fiddled with.

Your first-week checklist

  • ☐ One project created for the area of life you want to change

  • ☐ A head of staff chat, with a name and a role

  • ☐ Your idea talked through: who it is for, the problem, the offer, the price

  • ☐ A researcher and a website builder, each in their own named chat

  • ☐ Email connected, with a morning report and drafted replies

  • ☐ Drive sorted into folders, with an archive

  • ☐ Your domain, a professional email address and a first version of your website

  • ☐ A scheduling tool linked for social media

What a week can do

Do only this and within a week you will have a website, a professional address, a filing system, the beginnings of your first products and a team ready for whatever comes next.

For women starting a second act, that matters enormously. The barrier has never been a lack of ideas or experience. It has been time, money and the sheer weight of the admin. That weight is now optional.
