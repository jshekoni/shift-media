I have been on Substack for about two months. I have published at least 40 articles. I have 16 subscribers.

I post Notes every day, extracts from my articles. Most get no views, no likes, no comments. Then I scroll and see someone posting "How to make £10,000 on Substack", and it has a thousand comments. "Here's how I made £200,000 on Substack." "Here's how to write Notes that go viral." They fly.

It took me a while to see why. Nobody quite puts a finger on it, but the audience on Substack is not readers.

It's writers

Isn't Substack a site for people to read? Not really. Substack is a site people go to in order to write. Hence the problem.

Everyone in Waterstones is there because they want to read. Everyone buying a magazine or a newspaper is buying it to read.

Most people on Substack are there because they want to write.

Yes, someone will say, "Tell me what you've written, I want to read it." But that is a writer who wants to read. Not a reader who wants to read. And there is the problem.

Unless Substack starts advertising itself to the people outside it, so it becomes a Waterstones, a library or a newsstand where readers come for their reading, this will not change. Its why my article sat unread while "how to make it on Substack" collected a thousand comments.

It is also why I think Substack will never quite do what Instagram does. On Instagram, most people go to consume. On Substack, most people go to create.

The quilt or the kit

Think of Etsy. People go to Etsy to buy homemade things. They know when they arrive that they are there to consume what other people have made. So if you want to sell a homemade quilt, Etsy is the room.

But if your audience were people who wanted to make quilts, you would not sell them a quilt. You would sell them the tools, the kit, the pattern to make their own.

Substack is the second room. The people here want to make. Offer them a finished quilt and they walk straight past it.

Write for the room you're in

The biggest thing I have learned in my early days as a writer, an entrepreneur in my late forties, is this: write to the audience you have, not the audience you want.

The audience you want may not be in the room you're in. But write for the people who are, and you may get invited into the room where your people are.

Marketers call it top of funnel. You start with the widest audience you can reach, then, inside the writing, you narrow it to the people you actually came to speak to.

Mine are the thinkers. The ones who will work outside the box, who enjoy a different take on reality, who want to see things more clearly. I write the way I see: I notice something, I explain what it is, I come to a conclusion. I want the people it is meant for to leave with a sharper view, one that helps them with whatever they are building.

What I do is sharpen people's view. I bring things into focus. And I can take that to any audience I have. I can help writers bring into focus the platform we are actually on, the same way I can help a woman who is being abused bring into focus how it is happening, what it looks like, what it is doing to her, and so what the way out is.

So, writers. This one is yours.

Three things to take into your next piece

  1. Look at the room you are actually in, not the one you planned for. Who is reading, and why did they come?
  2. Write your first lines for them. Then let the piece narrow to the people you most want.
  3. Judge it by who stays, not by who you hoped would arrive.
